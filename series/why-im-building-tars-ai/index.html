The 1 to N series · Episode 01

Why I’m building TARS AI: making robotaxis pay at scale

Zero to one is what puts robotaxis on the road, but one to N is what makes them pay, and that is what we are building at TARS AI.

By Arunkumar Arunagiri · November 8, 2026

Prefer to watch? Open the video on YouTube

I’ve seen a robotaxi start as a bare frame on an assembly line. No pedals, no steering wheel. We took it from the factory to public roads.

Getting there took thousands of people across engineering, safety, legal, operations and so many other teams.

It took years of patient, relentless work by some of the smartest people I’ve ever worked with. Testing on the bench, on closed course, edge case after edge case, before a single public ride.

Two different problemsGoing from one to N is a different problem

That was the zero to one problem. Now the technology works on public roads and the industry is moving to scale. But going from one to N is a different problem.

One to NWhat it takes to run a thousand cars

What does it take to run a thousand cars? Depots that can charge and clean them. Service centers that keep them on the road. Roadside when the vehicle gets stuck. Remote operations when the car needs to phone a friend. And a dozen other functions riders never see at all.

Every new city is its own launch: new regulators, new roads, new rules. So how do you make all of that run?

The last two yearsTwo years around one question, laying the foundation for physical AI

I’ve spent the last two years building a company around that question. Talking with operators, customers and partners. Building models and agents, simulating the physical world, and testing them with the people doing the work to see what holds up. In the process, laying the foundation for physical AI in autonomous fleets.

What I foundThe car is autonomous. The operation around it is not

What I found was that the car is autonomous, but the operation around it is not.

Energy optimizes for energy. Maintenance optimizes for maintenance. Vendors and support do the same thing. Everyone’s piece works. The cost shows up in the handoffs.

The people doing the work are stretched thin. That works for a few hundred cars. It breaks at scale.

TARS AIThe goal: an operation as autonomous as the car

So I co-founded TARS AI. We are building AI agents that run the operations as one system, then robotics for the routine physical work. The goal: an operation as autonomous as the vehicles themselves.

Unit economicsScaling is not about more cars. One to N is what makes them pay

Scaling robotaxis isn’t about more cars. It’s a unit economics problem. How many hours a day each car is earning. How fast it turns around when it comes off the road. What every mile costs to run.

Zero to one is what puts these cars on the road. But one to N is what makes them pay.

That’s what we are building at TARS AI.
