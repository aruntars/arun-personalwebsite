Your board wants a robotaxi plan. Start with these six calls

If you run rental cars, rideshare supply or a corporate fleet, a robotaxi plan comes down to six connected calls, and one test you can run today.

By Arunkumar Arunagiri · November 12, 2026

Prefer to watch? Open the video on YouTube

Your board just asked for a robotaxi plan. You run rental cars, rideshare supply or a corporate fleet. Where do you start?

There are six calls to make, and they are all connected. The city shapes the partner. The partner shapes the sites. The sites shape the cost. And there is a way to pressure-test the plan before any robotaxi shows up.

Call 1 of 6Market entry: the operator is usually picked before the city is announced

In most cities, each AV partner works with one fleet operator. Avis runs Waymo’s depot in Dallas. Moove started in Phoenix and is now in Miami and Las Vegas too. And that operator is usually picked before the city is even announced.

So the conversation has to start early. What gets you in the room is how much faster you can help them launch.

Call 2 of 6Partner choice: it depends on what you already have

Avis and Hertz already have lots, chargers and people at airports. If you are asset-light, you will need two partners: an AV partner, and someone like Terawatt or Voltera for the sites and the power.

Call 3 of 6Sites: power close to demand is the rarest thing you can bring

The hardest sites to find are the ones with power, close to the demand hotspots. Those are rare, and AV partners know it. So if you already own one, that is worth more than anything else you bring to the table.

Call 4 of 6Mixed fleets: know when AVs need a site of their own

At first, AVs will likely share your lots, chargers and crews with your current fleet. That saves money and time to launch. But the AV partner wants capacity they can count on. So know how many AVs it takes before they need a site of their own.

Call 5 of 6Business case: decide what sits on your balance sheet and what partners carry

You are spending on depots, power and pilots long before the contract pays you back. Your existing business is paying for the AV launch. So decide what you put on your own balance sheet and what partners carry. Because if the launch slips, whatever you own sits idle.

Call 6 of 6Operating layer: uptime and turnaround ride on dozens of handoffs

Once the cars are running, the AV partner will judge you on uptime and turnaround time. Every minute a car sits idle in the depot is a minute it is not earning. Those numbers depend on dozens of handoffs across sites, fleets and vendors. Who ties it all together, and do you build that or buy it?

That is the plan. Now test it before any robotaxi shows up.

The pressure testDoes your operation keep up when the fleet grows tenfold?

Say you win the contract. The AV partner’s next question is about scale. If they send you 50 cars this quarter and 500 next year, does your operation keep up?

You do not need robotaxis to find out. Pick one of the sites you scored. Run the depot process there with the cars and people you already have, and measure every handoff.

Then pressure-test it: more cars, tighter windows, a failed charger mid-rush. Watch where it starts to slip.

That is your readiness case: what the site can handle, where it breaks and what it takes to fix it.

Now your plan comes with proof. You set the targets for the AV partner, instead of having them set for you.
