Why robotaxis need their own depots: power, deadhead and control

Every city already has places to charge, clean and fix cars, but almost none of it was built for robotaxis, so fleets need dedicated depots.

By Arunkumar Arunagiri · November 11, 2026

Prefer to watch? Open the video on YouTube

If you look around, every city already has places to charge, clean and fix cars. So why do robotaxi fleets need dedicated depots?

The short answer is that the infrastructure we see around us was built for cars with drivers. An autonomous vehicle needs more.

ChargingPublic chargers are shared with everyone, so a fleet cannot plan around them

That starts with chargers that operators can control. Public chargers are shared with everyone: private owners, ride-hailing drivers, corporate fleets. So a fleet cannot plan around them.

In a depotOperators control how much each car charges

In a depot, the operators control them, including how much each car charges. Too little and it comes back early, adding empty miles. Too much and it ties up a charger another car needs for the peak time.

Getting that balance right can matter as much as adding chargers.

Building a depotSites with several megawatts of power are hard to find

But building a depot isn’t easy. It needs several megawatts of power. Sites like that are hard to find.

Far from demandEvery trip to and from the depot is deadhead

Those sites also tend to sit in industrial pockets, far from the demand hotspots. So every trip to and from the depot is deadhead in terms of battery, time and wear and tear.

The car itselfA dirty or misaligned sensor changes what the car sees

Then there is the car itself. Cameras, lidars, radars. All of these sensors have to be cleaned and checked regularly, because a dirty or misaligned sensor can change what the car perceives.

No driver on boardAcross the fleet, cleaning is capacity

No driver means no one to catch a spill. One messy rider can take a car off the road mid-shift. So multiply that across the fleet, and cleaning becomes a capacity problem, not a chore.

Data offloadCars upload what they collected on the road

Depots also need a high-bandwidth data connection. The cars upload what they collected on the road, so engineers can review incidents and improve the driving software.

One place or three?The routine work happens in one place

If a car had to charge in one place, get cleaned in another, get inspected somewhere else, the time spent traveling, waiting and confirming it’s ready adds up fast. So the routine work tends to happen in one place: the depot.

Still earlyOperators want control of how the work gets done

On top of that, the technology and business are still early. Operators want to control how the work gets done, so they can change procedures quickly and protect every ride.

Why depots existAlmost none of the infrastructure was built for this

That’s why depots exist. Not because the infrastructure isn’t out there, but because almost none of it was built for this.

The real questionIt’s how many, where, and what each one does

So the question is not whether fleets need depots. It’s how many, where and what each one does.

Every site trades power and land against deadhead and peak readiness.

The fleet that gets that math right won’t just have more cars. They will have more cars on the road when the riders need them.
