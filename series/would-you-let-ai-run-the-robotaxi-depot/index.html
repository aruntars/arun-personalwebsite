Would you let AI run the robotaxi depot?

Operators asked if AI agents in a robotaxi depot would do the same thing every time, so people decide at first and every call goes into a decision log.

By Arunkumar Arunagiri · November 8, 2026

Prefer to watch? Open the video on YouTube

Would you let AI decide which robotaxi gets pulled off a charger in the middle of a rush?

What operators asked firstThe first question was about consistency, not how smart the agents were

When I started bringing agents into our depot OS, the first question operators asked wasn’t how smart the agents were. It was whether they would do the same thing every time in the same situation.

One real callEvery car waiting to hit 80% is holding a charger others need

Here is what one of those calls looks like. Cars are coming back faster than they can leave. The lot is jammed and only two chargers are free. Every car sitting there waiting to hit 80% is holding a charger the other car needs.

The agent suggestsRelease earlier, just for now

So the system suggests a change: release cars at 70% instead of 80% for the next 15 minutes, and shows exactly how many cars that would free up.

Now scale itHundreds of agents, working together like an orchestra

And that’s just one agent. Now imagine hundreds of agents like this one working together like an orchestra, coordinating every charger, every car, every shift towards one goal.

More uptime, better utilization, lower cost.

Keeping it predictableAt first, it’s the people: agents suggest, people decide

So what keeps hundreds of agents predictable? At first, it’s the people. In early deployments, there is a human in the loop. The agents suggest, people decide.

The decision logEvery call gets written down

That’s why every call goes into a decision log: what was suggested, why, and who approved it. If someone overrides it, they say why.

Lessons and signalsThe log becomes training data

Every override is a lesson. Every approval is a signal. Over time, the record is what you train the agents on.

Trust, earnedAs agents get it right more often, people step in less

As the agents get it right more often, people step in less. They stop approving routine calls and focus on the exceptions.

The strategic partThe data is yours alone. Nobody can sell it to you

Here is the strategic part. The data comes from your fleet, your sites and your people. Nobody can sell that to you.

The head startThe early starter ends up a year ahead

Two operators can buy or build the same software.

The one who starts early will be a year ahead, and the other cannot buy that time.
