A thousand-car robotaxi fleet could need 500 to 700 people. What they earn, what that costs per paid mile, and the lever that matters at scale.

Robotaxis don’t have drivers, but they need a lot of people.

A thousand-car robotaxi fleet could need 500 to 700 of them, across remote assistance, depot operations, customer support, roadside, repairs and collision.

The jobsThese are real jobs with real wages

Depot staff in San Francisco make about $26 to $30 an hour. Roadside assistance: $25 an hour. An operations supervisor: around $100,000 a year. A Waymo operations manager overseeing depots: about $170,000.

Blend it across every role, including the remote ones, and it comes to roughly $50,000 a year per person, before benefits.

The revenueWages alone take 57 to 80 cents of every paid mile

Now for the revenue. Let’s assume 20 paid trips per car a day, averaging 6 miles, at $20 a trip. That’s about $3.33 for every paid mile.

Roughly 57 to 80 cents of that goes to the people behind the fleet. And that’s before vehicle depreciation, insurance and infrastructure costs.

The leverThe question for scale is how much of the fleet each person can support

Most of them, about two-thirds, work in depots: charging, cleaning, inspecting, calibrating and maintaining cars.

So the question for scale isn’t how many people a fleet needs. It’s how much of the fleet each person can support.

Give them better tools, fewer wasted steps, less waiting on the next car, and each person keeps more cars on the road.

That’s how you grow robotaxi fleets profitably, without overwhelming the people keeping them running.
