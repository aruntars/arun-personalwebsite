Three chargers just died in your depot. The evening rush starts in an hour. Which robotaxi do you charge first?

Cars keep pulling in. A technician is on the fault, but there aren’t enough working chargers for every car.

The default ruleLowest battery first can add zero rides

The default rule is usually lower state of charge first. But what if one of those cars also needs a repair? It’s not going back out tonight anyway. Charging it now doesn’t add a single ride.

Meanwhile, another car just needs a quick top-up to cover the rush. Why not get that one charged first?

Right-size the chargeCharge for the rush, not for full

And not every car needs its usual charge target. Give each car what the rush needs, not a full state of charge, and the same chargers can serve more cars.

Charge at another site?Maybe. Three things decide it

What about sending cars to charge somewhere else? Maybe, but how far is it? Will a charger actually be free when you get there? And will they be back before the rush is over?

The real questionWhich car is back with riders soonest?

So the real question is: which car gets back to riders soonest?

That means weighing state of charge, work each car still needs, chargers at other sites, travel time, and where demand will peak in the next hour. All at once, while the rush is building.

The human limitYour best supervisor juggles all five, but only on their shift

Your best supervisor already thinks this way. The problem is that they cannot be there in every shift.

Where an AI agent helpsAn AI agent can re-weigh all five inputs every hour

That’s where an AI agent can help. Weighing it all at once, every hour, so you still have cars at the peak when something breaks.
